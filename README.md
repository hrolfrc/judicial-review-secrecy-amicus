# judicial-review-secrecy-amicus

# Judicial Review Cannot Be Defeated by Secrecy  
### Draft Amicus Curiae Brief

This repository contains a **draft amicus curiae brief** proposing a narrow constitutional rule:

> **Executive secrecy cannot be invoked in a manner that prevents courts from conducting meaningful judicial review of whether executive power itself is being exercised constitutionally.**

The brief addresses the structural question of whether executive privilege or the state-secrets doctrine may be used to withhold information when doing so would render judicial review illusory — particularly where substantial constitutional claims allege corruption, unlawful conduct, or undisclosed foreign influence affecting governmental decisionmaking.

## Purpose

This project is an exploratory legal draft intended to contribute to discussion of a constitutional principle grounded in established doctrine:

- *Marbury v. Madison* — judicial review requires courts to determine constitutionality.
- *United States v. Nixon* — executive privilege is qualified, not absolute.
- Structural constitutional safeguards require that no branch may place itself beyond judicial scrutiny.

The proposed rule does **not** abolish executive privilege or the state-secrets doctrine.  
Instead, it argues that secrecy must yield to procedures sufficient to preserve **meaningful judicial adjudication** when constitutional compliance itself is at issue.
